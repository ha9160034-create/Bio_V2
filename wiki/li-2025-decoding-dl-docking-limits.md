# Li et al. (2025) — Decoding the Limits of Deep Learning in Molecular Docking

**Summary**: A Chemical Science study systematically uncovering the physical invalidity, ligand memorization, cross-docking collapse, and virtual screening limitations of deep learning docking models.
**Sources**: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf
**Last updated**: 2026-11-01

---

## Citation and scope

Yue Li, Jiacai Yi, Hui Li, Kun Li, Fenghua Kang, Youchao Deng, Chengkun Wu, Xiangzheng Fu, Dejun Jiang, and Dongsheng Cao, "Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery," *Chemical Science* (2025), doi:10.1039/D4SC05395A (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).

The study executes a systematic, multidimensional interrogation into why deep learning (DL) molecular docking architectures frequently fail to translate high retrospective benchmark scores into actionable prospective drug discovery. The authors evaluate traditional physics-based engines (Glide SP, AutoDock Vina) against leading generative and regression DL docking algorithms (DiffDock, KarmaDock, QuickBind, SurfDock, Interformer, DiffBindFR, EquiBind, TANKBind, FABind) across Astex Diverse, PoseBusters, and DockGen datasets (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).

## The collapse of physical and chemical validity

The authors demonstrate that reporting pose prediction accuracy purely via root-mean-square deviation ($\text{RMSD} \le 2.0\text{ \AA}$) conceals catastrophic geometric and physical violations (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf):
- **KarmaDock validity failure**: KarmaDock exhibited **PB-valid rates of 0.00%** on both Astex Diverse and DockGen, and a marginal **0.23%** on PoseBusters, resulting in a **combined success rate ($\text{RMSD} \le 2\text{ \AA}$ + PB-valid) of 0.00% across all datasets** (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).
- **QuickBind validity failure**: QuickBind achieved PB-valid rates of only 1.18% (Astex), 1.17% (PoseBusters), and 0.00% (DockGen) (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).
- **Underlying structural violations**: Poses generated by these models routinely contained overlapping atomic coordinates, impossible covalent bond distances (<0.8 Å or >2.5 Å), strained bond angles, non-planar aromatic rings, and distorted chiral centers (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).
- **Physics-based contrast**: In sharp contrast, traditional engines (Glide SP and AutoDock Vina) operate on rigorous force fields and internal coordinate trees, maintaining **100% chemical and physical validity** (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).

## Generalization breakdown on novel pockets and DockGen

The benchmark revealed that DL docking models rely heavily on memorizing structural features from the PDBbind training dataset rather than mastering biophysical interaction principles (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf):
- On the DockGen dataset, which evaluates non-redundant, unseen protein receptor folds:
  - **SurfDock**: Raw $\text{RMSD} \le 2.0\text{ \AA}$ rate dropped from 91.76% (Astex) to 75.66% (DockGen), while combined success plummeted from **61.18% to 33.33%**.
  - **Interformer-Energy**: Combined success rate fell from **68.24% (Astex) to 34.39% (DockGen)**.
  - **DiffBindFR**: Combined success dropped from **65.88% (Astex) to 18.52% (DockGen)**.
- Performance degrades steeply as sequence and structural similarity to the training set decreases, demonstrating severe vulnerability to dataset leakage and out-of-distribution failure (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).

## The redocking versus cross-docking and apo-docking gap

The authors expose a critical methodological flaw in how DL docking models are trained and benchmarked (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf):
- Nearly all DL docking architectures are trained and tested on **holo crystal structures**, where the receptor binding site is already pre-accommodated to the co-crystallized ligand ("redocking").
- When evaluated under realistic **cross-docking** (docking ligands into alternate holo conformations) or **apo-docking** (docking into ligand-free unbound receptor structures), DL docking success rates collapse.
- DL models lack the physics-based mechanics required to resolve steric clashes with unrelaxed side chains, causing them to generate severe atomic overlaps when confronted with flexible side chains that have not been pre-shaped by the ligand (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).

## Failure in virtual screening and active ranking

In structure-based virtual screening evaluations designed to discriminate true active ligands from property-matched decoys:
- DL docking tools exhibited poor enrichment factors and often performed **no better than random selection**.
- The scoring surfaces of DL models fail to correlate with binding free energies and are easily misled by decoy molecules with high atomic contact counts (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).
- The authors conclude that classical physics-based methods (Glide and Vina) remain the benchmark standard for reliable prospective hit identification in drug discovery campaigns (source: Decoding the limits of deep learning in molecular docking for drug discovery.pdf).

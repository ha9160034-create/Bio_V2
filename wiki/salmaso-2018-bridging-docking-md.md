# Salmaso & Moro (2018) — Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics

**Summary**: A review examining the conceptual and methodological continuum between static molecular docking and dynamic molecular dynamics simulations in characterizing ligand–protein recognition.
**Sources**: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf
**Last updated**: 2026-11-01

---

## Citation and scope

Valentina Salmaso and Stefano Moro, "Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process: An Overview," *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 9:923 (2018), doi:10.3389/fphar.2018.00923 (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

The review synthesizes how computational pharmacology transitions from rapid, static approximations of binding (molecular docking) to dynamic, atomistic descriptions of the binding pathway and conformational landscape (molecular dynamics simulations).

## Evolution of molecular recognition models

The authors trace how computational paradigms mirror the historical evolution of biophysical recognition theories (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf):
1. **Lock-and-key model** (Fischer, 1894): Assumes complementary, rigid geometries; serves as the physical premise of early rigid docking algorithms.
2. **Induced-fit model** (Koshland, 1958): Posits that ligand binding drives mutual structural adaptations in the binding site; operationalized via flexible side chains and flexible docking.
3. **Conformational selection model** (Monod-Wyman-Changeux 1965; Burgen 1975; Tsai et al. 1999): Postulates that the native receptor samples an ensemble of pre-existing conformations in dynamic equilibrium, and the ligand selectively binds and stabilizes the most complementary sub-state.
4. **Extended conformational selection** (Csermely et al., 2010): Unifies both mechanisms, proposing an initial conformational selection event followed by an induced-fit structural relaxation of the bound complex (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

## Docking methodology and inherent boundaries

The authors review standard docking workflows (rigid, semi-flexible, and flexible) and their scoring categories (force-field, empirical, knowledge-based, and consensus) (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf). While docking achieves high throughput for screening chemical libraries, it suffers from severe intrinsic approximations:
- Omission of explicit solvent thermodynamics and water-mediated bridging networks;
- Incomplete sampling of protein backbone and global conformational flexibility;
- Inability to capture intermediate metastable states, desolvation barriers, or entry kinetics along the binding trajectory (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

To overcome rigid receptor limitations without exorbitant computational cost, the authors highlight **ensemble docking**, in which the ligand is docked into multiple representative conformational snapshots generated by prior molecular dynamics simulations or experimental ensembles (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

## Molecular dynamics and enhanced sampling strategies

Classical MD integrates Newton's laws of motion with empirical force fields (AMBER, CHARMM, GROMOS, OPLS) in explicit solvent (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf). Because biological binding events often occur over microsecond-to-millisecond timescales that exceed routine simulation lengths, enhanced sampling techniques are deployed (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf):
- **Replica Exchange MD (REMD)**: Parallel simulations across temperature or Hamiltonian ladders exchange configurations via Metropolis criteria, crossing high energy barriers.
- **Accelerated MD (aMD)**: Applies a continuous non-negative boost potential to the potential energy surface whenever the system potential drops below a defined threshold $E$, accelerating state transitions without predefined reaction coordinates.
- **Steered MD (SMD)**: Imposes time-dependent external pulling forces along a reaction vector to evaluate unbinding routes and reconstruct free energy profiles via Jarzynski's equality.
- **Random Acceleration MD (RAMD)**: Applies an external force vector in a randomly selected direction to the ligand center of mass, reorienting the vector when motion stalls, successfully uncovering unbinding channels and egress pathways.
- **Metadynamics**: Deposits repulsive Gaussian potential hills along selected collective variables (CVs) to discourage revisiting sampled states and reconstruct the free energy surface (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

## Supervised molecular dynamics (SuMD)

The authors detail Supervised Molecular Dynamics (SuMD), a specialized sampling protocol developed by Sabbadin and Moro (2014) to capture the entire binding recognition pathway without applying energetic or physical bias potentials to the molecular Hamiltonian (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).
- **Mechanism**: The simulation executes a succession of short classical MD steps (typically 600 ps windows). At the end of each window, the distance between the ligand center of mass and the receptor binding pocket center of mass is computed.
- **Supervision condition**: If the distance decreases, the window is accepted and the simulation continues. If the distance fails to decrease, the window is discarded, atom velocities are randomly reassigned, and a new window begins from the previous state.
- **Performance**: SuMD tracks the full unbinding-to-binding trajectory, identifying transient intermediate vestibules, desolvation steps, and induced-fit pocket adaptations on a nanosecond timescale (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

## Post-docking energetic refinement

MD plays an essential role in validating and refining docking poses (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf):
1. **Pose stability verification**: Unstable, artifactual docking poses drift significantly or detach during short (e.g., 10–50 ns) MD simulations, serving as an effective false-positive filter.
2. **Endpoint free-energy rescoring**: MM-PBSA and MM-GBSA calculate binding free energies by evaluating snapshots from MD trajectories, incorporating continuum electrostatics and non-polar solvation terms.
3. **Alchemical perturbation**: Rigorous Thermodynamic Integration (TI) and Free Energy Perturbation (FEP) utilize alchemical thermodynamic cycles to yield relative binding affinities with root-mean-square errors below 1 kcal/mol (source: Bridging Molecular Docking to Molecular Dynamics in Exploring Ligand-Protein Recognition Process An Overview.pdf).

The PDF is a compressed 3,648-page electronic edition; the printed page numbers in the table of contents run past 1,200 (source: Biochemistry Free For All 1.3_compressed.pdf).

**Ingestion status: partial.** Only the front matter (title, table of contents, foreword, copyright, and opening pages) and roughly the first 40 pages of body text were extracted and reviewed. The remaining chapters were not read into the wiki. Any claim about detailed biochemical content in this book should therefore be treated as **needing verification** against the original PDF before being cited.

## What the book is

*Biochemistry Free For All* is the second open educational resource textbook by the same authors, following *Biochemistry Free and Easy* (2012), which was downloaded over 175,000 times and adopted in courses across the United States. The project was supported by Oregon State University's Open Textbook initiative, OSU Libraries, OSU Press, and Open Oregon State (source: Biochemistry Free For All 1.3_compressed.pdf).

The book is explicitly **not** a reference work. The authors describe it as a core-concepts text for a course needing greater depth and breadth than *Biochemistry Free and Easy*, but not an encyclopedic tome. It is organized around the **Foundational Concepts** and learning goals of the American Society for Biochemistry and Molecular Biology (ASBMB) (source: Biochemistry Free For All 1.3_compressed.pdf).

Its pedagogical style is unconventional: short explanations, humor, rhymes and songs ("Metabolic Melodies"), videotaped lectures, interactive learning modules, and rotatable 3D molecules. The iBooks version supports the full multimedia; the PDF and Kindle versions retain hyperlinks but not all multimedia (source: Biochemistry Free For All 1.3_compressed.pdf).

## Why it matters for this wiki

The book supplies the biochemical background that underlies docking and molecular dynamics:

- **Water and buffers** — the solvent environment that dominates binding thermodynamics (see [[water-and-solvation]]).
- **Amino acids and protein structure** — the chemical basis of binding sites, hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic cores (see [[protein-ligand-interactions]] and [[receptor-preparation]]).
- **Catalysis and energy** — the thermodynamic and mechanistic framework for enzyme–ligand recognition (see [[binding-thermodynamics]] and [[binding-free-energy]]).
- **Nucleic acids, carbohydrates, and lipids** — the other major ligand and target classes, relevant beyond protein–small-molecule docking.
- **Membranes and transport** — the environment of GPCRs, channels, and transporters, which require membrane-aware simulation (see [[molecular-dynamics-simulation]]).

Because the detailed chapters were not ingested, the wiki does not yet draw specific facts from this book. It is listed as a source for orientation and for the curriculum scope, not for quantitative claims.
